# Stage 2 — Age engine v2: what it measures, how well, and what not to say

*16–17 Sept 2026. Decision D45 (`docs/00_MASTER_PLAN.md`). Plan: age engine v2, Parts A and C.*
*Figure: `figures/stage2/F2.10_age_calibration.png`. Numbers: `pipeline/drift/age_calibration.json`,
`pipeline/drift/out/twins/calibration_report.json`, `…/surrogate_eval.json`.*

## 1. The problem this fixes

Until D45, `origin.json` was the ensemble's **final step**. Whatever the rewind length, the origin sat
at the full horizon, so a slick six hours old and one two days old got the same origin and the same
window. `age.py` could already estimate an age (Huntington [2.1, 6.6] h against a true 2.8 h), but
nothing downstream used it, and all six bundles shipped `age_method: "none"`.

Moving the rewind to 72 h made this acute. On Jacksonville, the final-step origin at 72 h has
**r90 = 97 km**. That is far past the 40 km abstain threshold, so the hero case would name no vessels.

## 2. What the engine does

One hourly grid from 1 h to 72 h. Every estimator returns a likelihood on it. The output is a
posterior, and its 80 % highest-density interval is `age_hours`.

| Evidence | Reads | Model |
|---|---|---|
| **Shape** (E1 + E2, *mixed*) | length, width, bearing of the polygon vs forward runs released where the backward cloud was *t* hours earlier | our RK2 **and** OpenDrift OpenOil (evaporation, emulsification, dispersion, Stokes, mixing; 3 generic oils) |
| **Detectability** (E3) | fraction of oil still afloat at *t* | OpenOil |
| **Track width** (E4) | widest end of a ship's track, σ² = σ₀² + 2Kt | Okubo K |
| Weathering flag (display only) | centre vs edge contrast, from the bundle's own `sar_vv_vh.tif` | — |

The two readings of a slick, *patch* (released at a point) and *track* (laid by a moving ship), are
**averaged as posteriors**. `acute` uses patch, `chronic` uses track, `unknown` uses both. Two models of
the same observable are **mixed, never multiplied**. Suite 12 pins all three rules.

Two priors were changed on measurement:
- **Diffusivity follows Okubo's scale law** (×/÷3), no longer a flat band. With K free over three
  decades, age and K are degenerate: on Huntington the evidence moved the prior by 0.02 nats.
- **The release's initial size is unknown** (50–600 m, log-uniform). A fixed 200 m seed forced every
  wide slick to be old.

`run.py --age drive` pools the ensemble over the posterior: each hour's cloud is weighted by that
hour's probability. The grid, radii, abstain decision and window (`time_window_method: "age"`)
therefore all come from one pool. OceanDrift, with its own physics on, is pooled in at equal weight
(`model_mix`).

## 3. How accuracy was measured

**One real slick has a documented release time** (Huntington). One is not a validation set, so the
claim rests on **144 synthetic twins**: slicks with known ages, released within 12 km of each real
slick and run through **that case's own HYCOM + ERA5 fields**, 24 per field. They were turned into
SAR-like polygons and given to the engine as if Stage 1 had detected them.

- Half are generated by our model and half by OpenOil. A model is never scored only on its own output:
  - `cross_ours`: OpenOil truths, scored by our model alone
  - `cross_openoil`: our truths, scored by OpenOil alone
  - `mixture`: every twin, scored the way production scores
- Truth ages are log-uniform on 1.5–66 h, matching the prior. Truth diffusivity is Okubo ×/÷**4**,
  deliberately wider than the engine's ×/÷3, so the engine's prior is a little wrong on purpose.
  30 % of twins are ship tracks (3–7 m/s, 0.5–3 h discharges).
- Error terms, weights and the refusal threshold are fitted **leaving one field out**: each field's
  twins are scored by parameters fitted on the other five. A refusal scores as the prior, so
  "refuse everything" cannot win.

## 4. Results (held out, 144 twins)

| Setting | Twins | Answered | Refused | **80 % interval coverage** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **mixture (production)** | 144 | 70 | 74 | **0.81** |
| cross_ours | 72 | 21 | 51 | 0.91 |
| cross_openoil | 72 | 45 | 27 | 0.73 |

- **Calibration gate met:** 0.81 against a target band of 0.75–0.85.
- **Median |log₂(median / truth)| = 1.1** on answered twins, a typical error of about ×2.
- Fitted and stable across folds: σ_L 1.2 (1.2–1.6 across folds), σ_W 1.4, κ 0.5, weights shape
  0.5 / detectability 0.5 / track 0.25, refusal threshold 0.02 nats.
- **Per-field coverage varies** (answered / 24 in brackets): Farallones 0.93 (14), Gulf of Alaska
  1.0 (**4**), Jacksonville 0.92 (12), Jamnagar 0.93 (15), Mumbai 1.0 (9), **Huntington field 0.38
  (16)**. Calibration holds overall, not in every ocean, and a 1.0 on four answers is not evidence
  of anything.

**What the figure shows, stated plainly:**
- **Old slicks are dated too young.** Past about 20 h the posterior median levels off around 3–15 h,
  and the true-age CDF values bunch near 1. The intervals still usually reach the truth, which is why
  coverage holds, but the median should not be read as an age.
- **The engine refuses about half the time**, mostly young, small patches, where a 1 km slick looks
  the same at 1 h and at 15 h under any plausible diffusivity. That is physics, not a bug.
- **The track estimator is weak** (weight 0.25): its intervals usually reach the 72 h horizon.

### The learned surrogate (optional tier) does not ship
A quantile gradient-boosting model on twin features, held out by field: **80 % coverage 0.55**, log
score −4.60 against **−3.90 for the prior alone**, median |log₂ error| 0.89. It is sharper than the
physics and overconfident. With 144 examples it memorises the training fields. Its weight stays **0**,
exactly as the plan's gate requires.

## 5. The real cases

Regenerated 17 Sept 2026 with `publish_all.py --age drive --pool`. "Before" is `main` at `fafcfe2`
(24 h rewind, final-step origin). All six validate PASS.

| Case | Before: r90 km, window, age | Age 80 % (method) | Window (UTC), method | r50 / r90 km | Abstain | Models apart |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jacksonville | 31.1, convergence, none | **0.5–13.5 h** (combined) | 07-30 09:51 → 22:51, **age** | 14.7 / 33.9 | no | 0.46 km |
| Farallones | 8.8, bounded, none | **0.5–18.5 h** (combined) | 03-16 19:54 → 03-17 13:54, **age** | 5.5 / 12.4 | no | 1.47 km |
| Gulf of Alaska | 2.6, convergence, none | **0.5–11.5 h** (combined) | 05-16 04:27 → 15:27, **age** | 1.3 / 2.9 | no | 0.91 km |
| Huntington | 2.5, convergence, none | refused (gain 0.007) | 10-01 17:41 → 19:58, convergence | 3.8 / 7.1 | no | 7.67 km |
| Jamnagar | 3.7, bounded, none | refused (gain 0.005) | 48 h bracket, bounded | 14.1 / 21.3 | no | 27.34 km |
| Mumbai | 3.7, bounded, none | refused (gain 0.007) | 48 h bracket, bounded | 7.3 / 11.1 | no | 13.23 km |

**Reading it:**
- **Three of six cases now carry a measured age**, all three ship tracks (`chronic`), all young. The
  window replaces a fixed bracket with an interval that says *this slick is less than about half a day
  old*.
- **Without the engine, the 72 h rewind would have forced the hero case to abstain.** Jacksonville's
  final-step origin at 72 h has r90 97 km. Pooled over the calibrated posterior it was still 108 km,
  because an 11 % tail of old ages lies hundreds of kilometres up the Gulf Stream. Pooled over the
  stated 80 % window only, it is 33.9 km. §2 records why pooling over the window is the consistent
  choice, not a tuning.
- **Where both models pool over an age, they agree to within 1.5 km.** Where no age exists they are
  compared at the full horizon, and they part by 8–27 km. That is the horizon speaking, not the
  models, and it is the clearest argument for the age engine in the table.
- **Huntington, the only real slick with a known release time (2.8 h), is refused.** Before
  calibration the engine gave [0.5, 29.5] h, which contained the truth. The calibrated error terms are
  wider, and the evidence no longer clears the refusal threshold. The honest N = 1 statement is
  therefore *"on the one real slick with a documented release time, the calibrated engine declined to
  give an age."* It is not a hit.
- **The three refused cases keep the old behaviour**, a final-step origin at the rewind horizon, which
  is exactly what the plan specified for "no age". That is now a 72 h horizon, so their clouds are
  wider than the published 24 h ones (r90 3.7 → 11–21 km). Whether a refused case should pool over the
  prior instead is an open question for Akshat.

## 6. What to say, and what not to say

**Say:** *"Stage 2 reports an 80 % age interval, not a point. On 144 synthetic slicks with known ages,
run through our six cases' real ocean and wind fields and scored by a model that did not generate
them, the interval contained the true age 81 % of the time. When the evidence does not narrow the
age, the engine says so rather than guessing, which happens for about half of small young slicks."*

**Do not say:**
- "We estimate the age of a slick" with a single number. The median is not an estimate; old slicks
  read too young.
- "81 % accurate." It is interval coverage, not accuracy.
- Any per-case age as validated, except Huntington as **N = 1**.
- That OpenDrift "confirms" our age. The two models are mixed; where they agree it is two imperfect
  models agreeing.
- That the ML tier is part of the engine. It was tested and rejected.
